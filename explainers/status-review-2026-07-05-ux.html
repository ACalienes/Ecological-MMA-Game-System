The product, seen from the user's seat: what shipped today, what needs your taste

★ WHY THIS MATTERS

You said it yourself before class: the system isn't yet easy enough to consume for a student or a teacher learning it. So today the whole site got walked the way a real user would, on a desktop and on a phone, every finding verified against the actual rendered product. Six fixes shipped and verified. Three bigger design directions are drafted for you to pick from.

Three deeper directions, pick what resonates

DIRECTION A The mat-side game card

Every game page is a 4,600-pixel study document, brilliant for learning at home, unusable mid-class. Add a compact "run it now" view at the top of every game: the start position, the one constraint, both win conditions, one cue. A student on a phone between rounds sees the whole game in one screen; the long page stays below for study.

Buys: the single biggest student-value win, it makes the product usable in the gym, where the games actually get played. Also the natural seed of a paid-tier feature.

Costs: a template change rolled across 59 pages (scriptable), plus your sign-off on what the "essential five lines" are per game.

DIRECTION B Two doors: I'm training / I'm coaching

The nav treats a brand-new student and a coach identically: ten equal tabs, with 19 pages of academic theory sitting next to the games as peers. Restructure the entry so students get a clear journey (Mindset → a Path → Games → progress) and coaches get their own door (theory, session tools, lesson generator). Nothing gets deleted, it gets re-weighted.

Buys: solves the "who is this page for" confusion you flagged, makes the free student journey obvious, and cleanly frames a future coach-tier product.

Costs: a real information-architecture change, needs your call on what the student journey is, and a careful pass so deep links keep working.

DIRECTION C One recommender, not three

Get Your Plan, Learning Paths, and Game Finder all answer "what should I play next" from three different nav positions, and nothing tells a user they overlap. Crown Get Your Plan as the front door, fold Game Finder into it as its manual/browse mode, and reposition Learning Paths as the curriculum shelf the plan draws from.

Buys: removes the most confusing fork for a first-time visitor, one clear answer to the most important question a student has.

Costs: mostly nav/copy restructuring, the cheapest of the three directions.

Smaller items still open, no decision needed

Glossary auto-links: terms like shrimp, underhook, frame appear unlinked on game pages while the glossary defines them well. A scripted pass can link first-use per page.
The section-jump problem: game pages have no in-page table of contents because their headings are styled paragraphs, not real headings. Direction A largely absorbs this; otherwise a sticky jump-bar (Rules / Win / Levels / Recall) is the standalone fix.
A third of the library is WIP, and the newest pages are the densest. Not a bug, but worth a "field-tested first" default sort on the browse pages so newcomers land on polished games.
Search works, the browse pages are genuinely well organized, and the homepage's three CTAs give a clear start. None of this needs touching.
