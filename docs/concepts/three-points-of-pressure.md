<div class="emma-meta"><span>Core Concept</span><span>Control</span><span>Clinch &amp; Wall</span></div>

**Most people pin with their arms. Arms tire, and arms are two things a man can fight. Pressure applied at three separated points at once is a structure, and a structure is much harder to argue with.**

<p class="emma-hl">The three points are the <b>forehead</b> into the head or spine, the <b>shoulder and chest</b> across the top side, and the <b>hip</b> driving low. Together they take the top, middle and bottom of a body at the same time. He can usually solve one. He can rarely solve three.</p>

<div class="emma-band">
  <p class="emma-statement">Your head is a tool. <br><span class="accent">Most people carry it around like luggage.</span></p>
  <p class="emma-statement__sub">The forehead is the point almost nobody uses and the one that changes the position most. <b>Get there and be mean with it.</b></p>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Three Points</p>

<div class="emma-cards">
  <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🧠</div><div class="emma-card__t">Forehead, into the head or spine</div><p>Drive it in and keep it there. When he turns his head to look for an exit, take your forehead the other way and keep the pressure. It follows him rather than being shed.</p></div>
  <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">💪</div><div class="emma-card__t">Shoulder and chest, top side</div><p>Your centre mass into his top side. This is the point that stops him standing up straight and reclaiming his posture.</p></div>
  <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🦴</div><div class="emma-card__t">Hip, low</div><p>Hip to hip, driving. This is what stops the lower half from stepping out from under the top half, which is how most escapes actually begin.</p></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">Why Three, and Why Separated</p>

<div class="emma-flow">
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">One point</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">He turns away from it and it is gone</span></div>
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Two points close together</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">One movement can clear both, because they share a line</span></div>
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Three points, top, middle and bottom</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Clearing one leaves the other two, and the movement that clears it feeds the next</span></div>
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Structure instead of grip</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Frees a hand, because the position is being held by your frame rather than your arms</span></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-hl">The freed hand is the real prize. Once the structure holds him, <b>you are no longer choosing between holding and hitting</b>, which is the trade that decides most clinch exchanges. See <a href="../../games/clinch-holder/">Clinch Holder</a>, where that trade is the whole game.</p>

<p class="emma-glabel">Where It Comes From</p>

<p class="emma-hl">This is <b>Dagestani and Russian wrestling pressure</b>, and it is the clearest thing separating wrestlers who merely hold people from wrestlers who make holding unbearable. In MMA the <b>Diaz brothers</b> show the striking application: pin at the wall, transfer the forehead into the chest, and work from there. When the man stops trying to run and starts dealing with the punches, go back to the pin. The two states feed each other.</p>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Rhythm</p>

<div class="emma-flow">
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">He is trying to leave</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Pressure. Three points, forehead following his head</span></div>
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">He stops leaving and deals with the position</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Strike. The structure is holding him, so the free hand is available</span></div>
  <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">He reacts to the strikes and tries to leave again</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Back to pressure. The cycle is the control</span></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">Check, Not Checkmate</p>

<p class="emma-hl">Hip to hip on his side, one hand free, his weapons pointing away from you. <b>It is check, not checkmate.</b> He has nothing from there, but nothing has ended. Treating this position as a finish is how people get reversed out of it. Treat it as the place from which you choose what happens next: keep working, go to the back, start the takedown, or simply stay and make him carry you.</p>

<p class="emma-glabel">It Emerges: It Isn't Taught</p>

<p class="emma-hl"><b>Design philosophy:</b> forehead pressure appears on its own once a game makes <b>holding and hitting compete for the same two hands</b>. Players who cannot do both look for a third thing to hold with, and the head is what is available. Constrain the game so the trade is real, and this concept arrives as the answer rather than as an instruction. In its first session it was coached live four separate times before it was ever formally taught, which is the pattern to look for.</p>

??? note "Safety"

    <div class="emma-chips">
      <span class="emma-chip"><span class="emma-chip__i">🧠</span> Forehead into the head, shoulder or spine, never into the face or the throat</span>
      <span class="emma-chip"><span class="emma-chip__i">⬆️</span> Head <b>up</b> when you drive. Dropping the head so your crown leads is how necks get compressed, especially if your partner comes off the wall as you come in. If you are going to disappear, disappear into the wall, not into the floor</span>
      <span class="emma-chip"><span class="emma-chip__i">🩹</span> Any neck pain stops the round. Compression symptoms can arrive late</span>
      <span class="emma-chip"><span class="emma-chip__i">🤝</span> Sustained pressure is exhausting and easy to overdo on a smaller partner. Dial the load, not the structure</span>
    </div>
