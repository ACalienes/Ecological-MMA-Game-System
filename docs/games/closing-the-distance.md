<div class="emma-meta"><span>Open Space</span><span>Wrestling</span><span>Combined</span><span>Intermediate</span><span>Access → Transition</span></div>

The man who wants to grab you has almost nothing. The man who wants to stay free has almost everything, including the takedown. Close that gap anyway.

<div class="emma-arc">
  <div class="emma-arc__node"><span class="emma-arc__k">Start</span><b>Two fighters in open space at honest striking range, inside a marked perimeter.</b></div>
  <div class="emma-arc__sep">→</div>
  <div class="emma-arc__node"><span class="emma-arc__k">The Goal</span><b>The Closer wants a clinch above the hips that the Spacer cannot leave. The Spacer wants distance, or the legs.</b></div>
  <div class="emma-arc__sep">→</div>
  <div class="emma-arc__node emma-arc__node--finish"><span class="emma-arc__k">Finish</span><b>Clinch held through an escape attempt → Closer · Clean separation, redirect, or connection on the legs → Spacer · Leave the perimeter → loss.</b></div>
</div>

<div class="emma-band">
  <p class="emma-statement">There is no clock on the entry. <br><span class="accent">Collect enough to be sure, then go once.</span></p>
  <p class="emma-statement__sub">Reaching is how you get hit. You do not have to arrive quickly, you have to arrive. <b>Every step forward is priced, so buy the one that works.</b></p>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">What to Read</p>

<p class="emma-hl"><b>Attune to</b> the <i>retraction</i> of the jab, not its launch: the window opens as the arm comes home, and a lazy return opens it wider. Read <i>weight distribution through the lead foot</i>, because a Spacer who has just committed a cross has weight in front of their hips and cannot step back cleanly for a beat. Read <i>hip-to-hip distance</i> rather than glove distance, since gloves lie about how far away someone really is. For the Spacer, read <i>the quality of the forward momentum</i>: honest pressure can be met, but clumsy, over-committed pressure is the thing you steer past, and the difference is visible in whether their head has travelled ahead of their feet.</p>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Starting Position</p>

<div class="emma-setup">
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Players</span>Two, one Closer, one Spacer.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Range</span>Honest MMA striking range. Not the false close range that appears when nobody can punch.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Boundary</span>A marked perimeter, both stay inside.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Roles</span>Closer: no strikes at rung 1, a jab from rung 2. Spacer: a jab throughout, plus one resetting cross from rung 3, and the level change to the legs.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Start &amp; reset</span>"Go". The winner switches roles. Rotate groups with the winner staying in.</div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Matchup</p>

<div class="emma-matchup">
  <div class="emma-fighter emma-fighter--atk emma-has-pop" tabindex="0">
    <div class="emma-fighter__icon">🤝</div>
    <div class="emma-fighter__role">Closer (Forward pressure)</div>
    <p class="emma-fighter__obj">Trying to arrive at a clinch above the hips that holds when the other man tries to leave.</p>
    <span class="emma-pop">You do not want to box and you do not want to wrestle from out here. You want to be sticky. Use the jab to open a door, not to score. Control with the non-punching hand while the punching hand works. Do not reach: reaching is exactly when you get hit.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="emma-matchup__vs">VS</div>
  <div class="emma-fighter emma-fighter--def emma-has-pop" tabindex="0">
    <div class="emma-fighter__icon">📏</div>
    <div class="emma-fighter__role">Spacer (Keeping distance)</div>
    <p class="emma-fighter__obj">Trying to stay free, punish the entry, or drop under the hips and connect on the legs.</p>
    <span class="emma-pop">Build a wall of jabs so walking in costs something. Your cross is one shot and it has to be believed. If he charges badly, you do not have to meet him, you can steer him past you. And the moment he over-commits, the legs are there.</span>
  </div>
</div>

!!! note "Why the roles are not called offense and defense"
    The man applying pressure holds the fewest weapons here, and the man giving ground holds the most, including the takedown. Calling them offense and defense inverts what those words mean everywhere else and it reliably confuses the room. **Closer** and **Spacer** describe what each player is actually trying to do, which is the only thing either needs to remember.

<p class="emma-glabel">The Rules</p>

<div class="emma-chips">
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🤝</span> The Closer connects above the hips<span class="emma-chip__detail">Double underhooks, any cinch around the body above the waist, or a cinch above the elbows that stops the arms being pulled free. Nothing below the hips: the lower half of the body is not the Closer's to take in this game.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🦵</span> The Spacer connects below the hips<span class="emma-chip__detail">The level change is the Spacer's win, and it has to get under the hips onto the legs. The two players win in opposite halves of the same body, which is what keeps the exchange honest at both ends.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🧲</span> A clinch counts only if he cannot leave<span class="emma-chip__detail">The proof is his failed escape, not your grip. If he slips an arm out, or pops you and separates, you did not have it. Arrive and then hold it through one honest attempt to go.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">💣</span> One cross, then a full reset<span class="emma-chip__detail">From rung 3 the Spacer holds a single rear cross, head or body. It can follow a jab or arrive alone, but once thrown, the hands reset completely before another. No machine-gunning jab into cross. Your cross is your bomb: miss with it and you have handed over the entry.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">↩️</span> A redirect is a hold<span class="emma-chip__detail">If the Closer commits forward and the Spacer steers that momentum past themselves, the Spacer has kept their distance and won, even if the Closer ends up on the floor. Being put down by your own momentum is not a clinch.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">✋</span> Hand controls are always live<span class="emma-chip__detail">Wrist control, collar ties, framing and pummelling never switch off, at any rung, for either player. Adding strikes does not remove the hands.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">⬛</span> Stay inside the perimeter<span class="emma-chip__detail">Both feet out loses the exchange, whoever you are. Without it the Spacer solves the game by walking backwards forever, which is not a solution to anything.</span></span>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">How to Win</p>

<div class="emma-outcomes">
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Closer</span> Clinch above the hips, held through one real escape attempt → switch roles.<span class="emma-pop">The observable proof: he tries to get out and fails. Hold roughly three seconds of honest resistance, or hold it through a visible attempt to slip the arm or shove off, whichever comes first. A grip he walks out of never happened.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Spacer</span> Connect below the hips on the legs → switch roles.<span class="emma-pop">A real level change that arrives under the hips with hands on the legs. A sprawl or a clean break means no count. Feinting the level change does not score.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Spacer</span> Redirect the committed entry and stay free → switch roles.<span class="emma-pop">He charged, you steered it past you, and you are still standing at range. Counts whether he stays up or goes down. This is the ruling to watch, see the note in Constraints-Led analysis.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Spacer</span> Break clean out of an attempted clinch → the exchange continues.<span class="emma-pop">Not a switch, a continuation. He got hands on you and you got out before it set, so the problem is live again from range. This is what stops the Closer from claiming every touch.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--lose emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Loss</span> Both feet leave the perimeter.<span class="emma-pop">Instant loss regardless of position, for either player. It is the constraint that keeps the cage edge real.</span></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Levels</p>

<div class="emma-stepper">
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">1</span><b>Nothing in your hands</b>The Closer has no strikes at all.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">Closer: no punches, no kicks, hand controls and footwork only. Spacer: a jab, and the level change to the legs. This rung is entirely about patience and reading, because the Closer has no way to make anything happen except by arriving at the right moment.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">2</span><b>The Closer earns a jab</b>One tool, used to open a door.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">The Closer gets a jab. It is not there to win exchanges, it is there to make the Spacer react so the entry has somewhere to go. Very Royce Gracie: the man coming in does not want a boxing match, he wants a grip.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">3</span><b>The bomb</b>The Spacer earns one resetting cross.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">A single rear cross, head or body, with a full reset after. It prices the walk-in, and it prices lazy jab-peppering. Its cost is built in: throw it and miss, and the retraction is the best entry the Closer will get all round.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">4</span><b>The wall joins in</b>The environment goes live.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">Begin one body length off the wall. The Closer can now drag the Spacer onto it, and the Spacer has to keep their back off it. The clinch that could be walked out of in open space is much harder to leave once there is a wall behind you.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">5</span><b>Finish it</b>The takedown is restored.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">The Closer may now convert the held clinch into a takedown or a wall pin rather than stopping at the connection. Only run this rung when the group has the position first, because a win condition reached from a bad position is not a win.</span></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">Recall Check</p>

<div class="emma-recall">
  <p class="emma-recall__lead">Test yourself before moving on. Answer out loud, then reveal what good looks like.</p>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> You are the Closer and you have no weapons. Should you rush in and grab?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a"><b>No.</b> There is no clock on the entry. Rushing is exactly what the Spacer's cross and level change are waiting for. Your job is to collect enough information that when you finally go, it works. Step in, see what comes back, adjust, and go once with high confidence rather than five times with low.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> You got both hands on him, then he shrugged you off. Did you win?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a"><b>No.</b> The clinch is proven by his failure to leave, not by your grip arriving. If he slipped out or popped you off and made space, it did not count and the exchange is still live. The whole point of the position is that he cannot go.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> He charged you, you steered him past, and he fell over. Who won?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a"><b>You did, as the Spacer.</b> Your job was to not be clinched and to still be free. He is on the floor because of his own momentum, and he never got hold of you. Note that this ruling is deliberately narrow: it is a Spacer win here, and we are not yet treating it as a general rule across every clinch-entry game.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> Why can the Closer only take the top half and the Spacer only the bottom half?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a">Because it makes both jobs real at once. If the Closer could shoot, this would just be a takedown game. If the Spacer could clinch, they would stop having to keep distance. Splitting the body at the hips gives each player one honest route and forces them to actually solve the range problem between them.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> Should you apply more forward pressure?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a"><b>Only as much as buys you the reactions you want.</b> More pressure is not more virtue. It raises your risk of being taken down or steered past, in direct proportion. Use enough to make him respond, use the jab to open the door, and keep the rest in your pocket.</div>
  </details>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">Go Deeper</p>

??? note "Task focus &amp; coaching cues"

    <p class="emma-sub">Each role's job</p>

    <div class="emma-cards">
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🤝</div><div class="emma-card__t">Closer</div><p>Tight defence, calculated approach, one committed entry. Control with the free hand and punch with the other. Arrive above the hips and make him fail to leave.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">📏</div><div class="emma-card__t">Spacer</div><p>Volume on the jab so walking in costs. One believable cross. Read committed pressure and steer it. When his head travels ahead of his feet, take the legs.</p></div>
    </div>

    <p class="emma-sub">Coaching cues</p>

    <div class="emma-cards">
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">⏳</div><div class="emma-card__t">There is no time limit on the entry</div><p>The most common Closer error is treating the round like a race. Say it plainly: you do not need to get there quickly, you need to get there. Gather data, keep a tight guard, and act when the confidence is high. A failed entry is worse than no entry, because the failure is what gets punished.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🚫</div><div class="emma-card__t">Stop reaching</div><p>Students with no weapons reach out with both hands and get popped for it. Reaching announces the entry and gives away the arms. The hands stay home until the moment is bought and paid for.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🔫</div><div class="emma-card__t">The jab is a force field, not a scoring tool</div><p>For the Spacer, volume is the job. Pump it so that anyone walking in eats two or three on the way. For the Closer, the jab exists to make him blink so the feet can travel, and nothing more.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🎭</div><div class="emma-card__t">Volume makes feints work</div><p>Feints only land on someone who has been made to respect the real thing. Saturate the space with real shots first, then the feint, then the level. In that order, or the feint buys nothing.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">✌️</div><div class="emma-card__t">One hand controls, one hand punches</div><p>Discovered live by a student rather than taught. The non-punching hand takes a wrist or a tie while the punching hand works, which occupies the Spacer's defence and buys the step. Worth letting the room find on their own before naming it.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🎯</div><div class="emma-card__t">Name the goal out loud, often</div><p>This game confuses people more than most, because the man pressing forward is the one with nothing. Before each round have both players state their own win condition in one sentence. It costs ten seconds and prevents most of the stoppages.</p></div>
    </div>

??? abstract "Constraints-Led analysis"

    <p class="emma-sub">Constraints → Affordances</p>

    <div class="emma-flow">
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Closer stripped of strikes</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Entry has to be bought with timing and hand fighting rather than covered by punching</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Closer capped above the hips, Spacer capped below</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Two live routes on one body, so neither player can collapse the game into a pure boxing or pure wrestling problem</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">The clinch is proven by his failed escape</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Rewards a grip that holds under load instead of a grip that merely arrives</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">One cross with a full reset</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Prices forward motion while building the punish window into the weapon itself, so overcommitment is self-correcting</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Redirect counts as keeping distance</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Opens a second, non-collision answer to pressure and rewards reading the quality of the charge</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Perimeter</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Removes infinite retreat, so distance has to be manufactured rather than taken</span></div>
    </div>

    <p class="emma-hl">The defining feature is the <b>complementary win zone</b>. The two players score in mutually exclusive halves of the same body, in the same exchange, which is unusual in the library and is what stops either from solving the game by changing what game it is. The second feature is the <b>self-punishing weapon</b>: the Spacer's cross does not need an external penalty because its own miss is the Closer's best entry, which is a cleaner design than a rule that says "do not overuse this".</p>

    <p class="emma-hl"><b>Open ruling, pending the mat:</b> whether the redirect win generalises to the rest of the clinch-entry family, or stays specific to the version where the Closer is weapon-poor, is deliberately unresolved. Run it here, watch whether it degrades into flinching and pulling rather than genuine steering, and decide from that. Do not backport it until it has been tested.</p>

    <p class="emma-sub">What athletes read</p>

    <div class="emma-cards">
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">👁️</div><div class="emma-card__t">Visual</div><p>Jab retraction and how lazily the hand comes home → the size of the entry window. Head travelling ahead of the feet → a charge that can be steered, or legs that can be taken.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">✋</div><div class="emma-card__t">Haptic</div><p>Load arriving through a tie or a frame → whether he is set or reaching. Once connected, whether his weight is coming to you or leaving, which decides hold against re-clinch.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🧭</div><div class="emma-card__t">Proprioceptive</div><p>Own hip-to-hip distance rather than glove distance, and where the perimeter sits behind the heels.</p></div>
    </div>

    <p class="emma-sub">What we measure (order parameter)</p>

    <p class="emma-hl">The <b>ratio of committed entries to successful ones</b>. Early on students enter often and cheaply and get punished; the game has worked when entries become rarer, later, and far more likely to stick. Track entries attempted against clinches that survive an escape attempt, and watch whether the Closer's hands stay home between attempts.</p>

    <p class="emma-sub">Representativeness</p>

    <p class="emma-hl"><b>Models:</b> the grappler-versus-striker problem as it actually appears in a fight, where the man who wants the clinch has to cross the other man's best range to get it, and pays for every mistake with a takedown or a clean shot. The one-cross rule keeps the distance honest so the entry problem stays real.</p>

    <div class="emma-meta"><span>Closer above the hips</span><span>Spacer below the hips</span><span>the clinch must survive an escape</span></div>

    <p style="font-size:.82rem;color:var(--fog-dim);margin:.7rem 0 0;">Inverts <a href="../pressure-to-clinch/">Pressure to Clinch</a>, where the attacker escalates strikes and the defender denies. Here the man closing is the one disarmed. Sits opposite <a href="../clinch-denial/">Clinch Denial</a>, which is the pure-denial version of the Spacer's job, and feeds directly into <a href="../fifty-fifty-clinch/">50/50 Clinch</a> and <a href="../clinch-holder/">Clinch Holder</a> once the grip is made.</p>

    <p class="emma-sub">First-run observations (2026-08-21)</p>

    <ul class="emma-checklist">
      <li>Roles had to be re-explained four separate times in an hour under the offense and defense labels, which is why this page names them Closer and Spacer</li>
      <li>Closers reached with both hands and were punished for it, consistently, at every rung</li>
      <li>The patience framing ("no time clock, collect enough data") changed behaviour more than any rule adjustment</li>
      <li>A student independently discovered the control-hand and punch-hand split, which is the game producing the solution rather than the coach</li>
      <li>Run in an odd-numbered rotation, which added to the role confusion; pair the group evenly if you can</li>
      <li>Run without a marked perimeter live, the perimeter is our addition for consistency with the rest of the library</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="emma-sub">Readiness to progress</p>

    <ul class="emma-checklist">
      <li>Enters once, well, rather than repeatedly and cheaply</li>
      <li>Keeps the hands home until the entry is bought</li>
      <li>Holds the clinch through an honest escape attempt rather than claiming the touch</li>
      <li>As Spacer, throws the cross like it is real and does not spend it carelessly</li>
      <li>Reads a bad charge and steers it instead of meeting it head on</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="emma-sub">Warning signs</p>

    <div class="emma-warns">
      <span class="emma-warn">Reaches with both hands from outside range</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Treats the round as a race and charges repeatedly</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Counts a grip the partner walked straight out of</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Drifts to a range nobody would really stand at because the other man cannot punch</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Throws the cross soft, so it prices nothing</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Announces a read to the partner instead of using it</span>
    </div>
