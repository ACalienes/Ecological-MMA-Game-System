<div class="emma-meta"><span>Skill Isolation</span><span>Striking</span><span>Combined</span><span>Intermediate</span><span>Escalating</span></div>

One game whose bar keeps rising. The defender starts by winning with a check, then has to check **or** return fire, then has to check **and** return fire. By the top rung a check buys you nothing on its own, and the last clean kick in the exchange is the only thing that wins.

<div class="emma-arc">
  <div class="emma-arc__node"><span class="emma-arc__k">Start</span><b>Two fighters at kicking range inside a marked perimeter; offense holds a lead hand and a rear leg, nothing else.</b></div>
  <div class="emma-arc__sep">→</div>
  <div class="emma-arc__node"><span class="emma-arc__k">The Goal</span><b>Offense lands a clean leg kick; defense checks it and answers with one of their own.</b></div>
  <div class="emma-arc__sep">→</div>
  <div class="emma-arc__node emma-arc__node--finish"><span class="emma-arc__k">Finish</span><b>Defense checks and returns → switch · Offense lands clean → continue, no reset · Last clean kick wins.</b></div>
</div>

<div class="emma-band">
  <p class="emma-statement">Stopping it isn't the answer. <br><span class="accent">The answer is the one you send back.</span></p>
  <p class="emma-statement__sub">You want him to kick. It is the only thing that can hand you the win. <b>Move away and you have talked yourself out of the game.</b></p>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">What to Read</p>

<p class="emma-hl"><b>Attune to</b> <i>τ (time-to-contact)</i> on the rising shin and the <i>hip and shoulder pre-rotation</i> that launches it, the same read that times any check. What this game adds is the layer underneath: the <i>weight distribution</i> the offense is manufacturing with the lead hand. A jab or a lead hook that pushes weight onto the lead leg <b>tells you a kick is being set up</b>, and tells the kicker their target has been loaded. At the top rungs you also read <i>stance level</i>, upright with the eyes on your legs specifies a kick, a dropping head and hips specifies a shot, and the two demand different feet.</p>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Starting Position</p>

<div class="emma-setup">
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Players</span>Two, one on offense, one on defense.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Range</span>Kicking range, both on their feet, honest MMA distance.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Boundary</span>A marked perimeter, both stay inside.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Roles</span>Offense: lead hand and rear leg only. Defense: no strikes at the first rung, earning weapons as the ladder climbs.</div>
  <div class="emma-setup__item"><span class="emma-setup__k">Start &amp; reset</span>"Go". Roles switch only when the defense wins. A winning offense keeps going without a pause. Winner stays in when rotating groups.</div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Matchup</p>

<div class="emma-matchup">
  <div class="emma-fighter emma-fighter--atk emma-has-pop" tabindex="0">
    <div class="emma-fighter__icon">🦵</div>
    <div class="emma-fighter__role">Offense (Landing)</div>
    <p class="emma-fighter__obj">Trying to land a clean rear-leg kick above the knee, using the lead hand to build and then break a pattern.</p>
    <span class="emma-pop">The lead hand is not for damage, it is for moving his weight and his feet. Jab, hook to load his lead leg, then kick. Feint far more than feels natural, with the hand, the level, and the kick itself. Land clean and you keep going, so do not celebrate and reset.</span>
  </div>
  <div class="emma-matchup__vs">VS</div>
  <div class="emma-fighter emma-fighter--def emma-has-pop" tabindex="0">
    <div class="emma-fighter__icon">🛡️</div>
    <div class="emma-fighter__role">Defense (Answering)</div>
    <p class="emma-fighter__obj">Trying to check the kick and send one straight back, inside the same exchange.</p>
    <span class="emma-pop">Stay in range and stay spring-loaded. You need his kick to happen, so do not travel. Weight ready on the leg you will pick up, rotate the knee out so the shin takes it. When you get the rear straight, throw it like you mean it or it does nothing.</span>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Rules</p>

<div class="emma-chips">
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🦵</span> Offense: lead hand and rear leg only<span class="emma-chip__detail">No rear hand, no lead leg. Stance switching is legal, but the constraint follows the new stance: whatever is now your lead hand is the hand you have. Inside or outside, open or closed stance, all legal.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🎯</span> Target: above the knee, below the hip<span class="emma-chip__detail">The thigh. No calf kicks at the lower rungs, they can be opened up once the group is landing cleanly. Nothing to the knee joint itself.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🦴</span> Shin, never instep<span class="emma-chip__detail">A kick landed with the toes or the top of the foot does not count, and the kicker calls it on themselves. Instep contact is a red flag on your distancing, not a scored kick. Kick a knee or an elbow with the top of your foot and you will be hurt for weeks.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">⚖️</span> The burden of proof sits on the kicker<span class="emma-chip__detail">Checking is a vague event, landing is a clean one, so they are not judged the same way. A check that felt imperfect but stopped the kick counts as a check. A kick that did not land cleanly is the kicker's problem. Be harsh on your own kick and generous with their check.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">🔁</span> No reset on a win that does not switch<span class="emma-chip__detail">If the offense lands and stays on offense, the exchange does not stop. No high five, no step back, no restart. Pausing every time you succeed trains you to hand back the initiative you just earned.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">👊</span> The rear straight cannot win<span class="emma-chip__detail">From rung 3 the defense holds a single rear straight, head or body. No hooks, no uppercuts, no overhands. It scores nothing. It exists only to make walking in and peppering jabs expensive, and it only works if it is thrown hard enough to be believed.</span></span>
  <span class="emma-chip emma-chip--info" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-chip__i">⬛</span> Stay inside the perimeter<span class="emma-chip__detail">Play happens inside a marked perimeter. Leaving it loses the exchange. It also stops the defense from solving the game by simply travelling, which is not a solution.</span></span>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">How to Win</p>

<div class="emma-outcomes">
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Defense</span> Check the kick and land one back → win the exchange and take the offense.<span class="emma-pop">At the top rung this is the only full win. Check, then return a clean leg kick before the window shuts. Checking alone no longer scores.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Defense</span> Eat the kick but return a clean one → still a win.<span class="emma-pop">You did not get there in time, but you answered. The rule that makes it work: the last person to land a clean kick wins the exchange, no matter who started it.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--win emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Offense</span> Land clean and uncountered → continue on offense.<span class="emma-pop">A clean landed kick that draws no answer keeps you where you are. Keep the pressure on, the exchange does not reset.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-outcome emma-outcome--lose emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-outcome__tag">Loss</span> Get out-kicked in the exchange, or leave the perimeter.<span class="emma-pop">If they land after you land, you lost the exchange and the roles switch. Crossing the perimeter loses it outright.</span></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">The Levels</p>

<div class="emma-stepper">
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">1</span><b>The check wins</b>Defense has no strikes at all.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">Offense: lead hand and rear leg. Defense: no strikes, but hands and guard are live for hand controls, and footwork is free. Defense wins by checking. This rung teaches the perch, and it teaches that you need his kick to happen.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">2</span><b>Check or return</b>A second door opens for the defense.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">Same game. Defense now also wins by eating the kick and giving one back. The last clean kick wins the exchange, which is the rule that makes the whole ladder work.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">3</span><b>The disincentive</b>Defense earns one rear straight.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">A single rear straight, head or body, that cannot win the game. It exists to price the offense's distance, because with no punches on the other side the offense drifts to a range no one would ever really stand in. Gold star at this rung: check and return.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">4</span><b>Check and return</b>The check stops being a win.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">The top rung of the core game. Checking alone scores nothing now. You must check and kick back, or eat it and kick back. Because the last clean kick always wins, the exchange can ladder indefinitely, which is the point: whatever is happening, you are answering.</span></div>
  <div class="emma-step emma-has-pop" tabindex="0"><span class="emma-step__n">5</span><b>The shot goes live</b>Offense can win on the legs.<span class="emma-pop emma-pop--l">Offense keeps everything and adds a win by connecting on the legs. A sprawl or a break means no count. The reciprocal is the good half: showing the level change is an invitation for the defense to counter with a kick, so the shot fake is no longer free. Now the defense has to read kick threat against shot threat and pick its feet accordingly.</span></div>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">Recall Check</p>

<div class="emma-recall">
  <p class="emma-recall__lead">Test yourself before moving on. Answer out loud, then reveal what good looks like.</p>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> Your only job is to check. So why not stay far away and keep moving?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a">Because <b>his kick is the only thing that can win you the exchange</b>. Travel and you reduce the number of kicks thrown at you, which reduces your chances to win. You also cannot move and check at the same time. Stay in range, stay spring-loaded, and let the kick come to you.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> Why does the defense get a rear straight it cannot score with?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a">To restore a cost the constraint deleted. With no punches on the defensive side, the offense can stand at a range it would never really stand at. Telling people to respect the range is an instruction they will forget. A punch that hurts to walk into is a <b>constraint</b>, and it fixes the range without anyone being reminded.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> You land a clean kick. What happens next?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a"><b>Nothing stops.</b> You are still on offense, so you keep working. Resetting after every success trains the habit of giving back the initiative the moment you earn it, which is the opposite of what a fight rewards.</div>
  </details>
  <details class="emma-recall__item">
    <summary><span class="emma-recall__q">Q</span> Their check looked sloppy but your kick did not land. Who won?</summary>
    <div class="emma-recall__a"><b>They did.</b> The two events are not judged to the same standard on purpose. A check is a vague thing to see, so it gets the benefit of the doubt; landing is clean and obvious, so it carries the burden. This keeps the game honest without a referee.</div>
  </details>
</div>

<p class="emma-glabel">Go Deeper</p>

??? note "Task focus &amp; coaching cues"

    <p class="emma-sub">Each role's job</p>

    <div class="emma-cards">
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🦵</div><div class="emma-card__t">Offense</div><p>Use the lead hand to manipulate weight and feet, not to score. Build a rhythm, then break it. Land with the shin above the knee. Keep going when you win.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🛡️</div><div class="emma-card__t">Defense</div><p>Hold your ground in range, weight ready on the checking leg, knee rotated out. Check, then answer immediately. Make the rear straight believable.</p></div>
    </div>

    <p class="emma-sub">Coaching cues</p>

    <div class="emma-cards">
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🎯</div><div class="emma-card__t">Play this game, not MMA in general</div><p>The most common failure is a student who understands the rules and still plays a generic fight. Ask them out loud: given these rules, what is the cheapest way for you to win? Movement, range and posture should all follow from that answer, not from habit.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">✋</div><div class="emma-card__t">The lead hand moves weight</div><p>Not a damage tool here. A lead hook that puts weight into the body loads their lead leg, and a loaded leg is a leg that cannot be picked up. Jab, hook, kick. Against someone who parks with the leg already up, the hand is how you break their balance and take the timing back.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🎭</div><div class="emma-card__t">Feint far more than feels natural</div><p>Feint the hand, feint the level, feint the kick, feint twice. Elite fighters feint many times over before they commit to the strike that counts. A feint with a weapon your opponent knows you do not have will not be bought, so feint with what is live.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">📈</div><div class="emma-card__t">Use the pattern, do not announce it</div><p>Students notice "you kick every time I throw the cross" and then say it out loud to their partner. That read is worth something only if it is spent: throw the cross expecting the kick, or feint it and take the leg they just committed.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🔊</div><div class="emma-card__t">A disincentive nobody respects is not a constraint</div><p>Thrown soft, the rear straight is decoration. If they walk in anyway, it needs more snap. That is calibration, not ego: the read is "I threw it and nothing changed", and the response is to dial up until the behaviour changes.</p></div>
    </div>

??? abstract "Constraints-Led analysis"

    <p class="emma-sub">Constraints → Affordances</p>

    <div class="emma-flow">
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Offense capped at lead hand + rear leg</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Forces the hand to be used as a manipulation tool rather than a scoring tool</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Defense wins only when the kick is thrown</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Makes retreat self-defeating, the defender has to seek the threat instead of avoiding it</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">The bar rises rung by rung</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">The previous win condition expires, so a solved problem cannot be re-solved for credit</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Non-scoring rear straight</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Restores the cost of closing distance that the weapon ban removed, as a constraint rather than a reminder</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Last clean kick wins</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Turns a single exchange into an open ladder, rewarding the answer over the flinch</span></div>
      <div class="emma-flow__row"><span class="emma-flow__from">Showing the level change invites a counter</span><span class="emma-flow__arr">→</span><span class="emma-flow__to">Prices the free shot fake and forces a genuine kick-or-shot read</span></div>
    </div>

    <p class="emma-hl">The defining feature is the <b>expiring win condition</b>. Most of the library adds options as it scales; this game removes them, so the same rep gets harder without the task changing shape. Rung 3 is also a clean worked example of turning an <b>instruction into a constraint</b>: the range problem was first coached verbally and then designed away, which is the move to prefer whenever a cue has to be repeated.</p>

    <p class="emma-sub">What athletes read</p>

    <div class="emma-cards">
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">👁️</div><div class="emma-card__t">Visual</div><p>τ of the rising shin and the hip and shoulder that launch it → when to pick the leg up. Stance level and where the eyes sit → kick threat or shot threat.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">✋</div><div class="emma-card__t">Haptic</div><p>Weight arriving through a jab or a lead hook → which leg has just been loaded, for both players.</p></div>
      <div class="emma-card"><div class="emma-card__i">🧭</div><div class="emma-card__t">Proprioceptive</div><p>Own base and where the feet finish after a kick → whether an answer is even available, or whether the kick has left you jammed and out of position.</p></div>
    </div>

    <p class="emma-sub">What we measure (order parameter)</p>

    <p class="emma-hl">Whether the <b>check and the return arrive as one action</b> rather than two decisions with a gap between them. Track exchanges won by check-and-return against checks with no answer, and count the pauses after a winning offensive kick. When the return stops being a separate thought, and the post-win pause disappears, the game has done its work.</p>

    <p class="emma-sub">Representativeness</p>

    <p class="emma-hl"><b>Models:</b> the leg-kick exchange as it really runs, where stopping the kick is only half the job and the fighter who answers last owns the exchange. The non-scoring straight keeps the distance honest so the kicking problem stays a real one.</p>

    <div class="emma-meta"><span>Rear leg only</span><span>above the knee, shin only</span><span>last clean kick wins</span></div>

    <p class="emma-sub">First-run observations (2026-08-14)</p>

    <ul class="emma-checklist">
      <li>Defenders travelled far too much on rung 1, traceable to carryover from recent footwork games; the fix was the "you need his kick" framing, not a new rule</li>
      <li>The post-win reset had to be corrected three separate times, which is what promoted it from a cue to a rule</li>
      <li>The rear straight was thrown soft by most of the room and was consequently ignored, until it was called out</li>
      <li>Two students hurt the top of the foot on a knee within the same hour; the shin-not-instep rule is not a formality</li>
      <li>Run without a marked perimeter live, the perimeter is our addition for consistency with the rest of the library</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="emma-sub">Readiness to progress</p>

    <ul class="emma-checklist">
      <li>Holds range under pressure instead of managing the problem with footwork</li>
      <li>Returns fire without a pause after checking</li>
      <li>Lands with the shin, and calls their own bad kicks honestly</li>
      <li>Keeps working after a successful offensive kick rather than resetting</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="emma-sub">Warning signs</p>

    <div class="emma-warns">
      <span class="emma-warn">Circles away and waits, reducing the kicks thrown at them</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Celebrates and resets after landing</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Throws the rear straight without intent, so it changes nothing</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Lands on the instep and counts it</span>
      <span class="emma-warn">Steps off to a strange angle to kick and cannot return fire from where they land</span>
    </div>
