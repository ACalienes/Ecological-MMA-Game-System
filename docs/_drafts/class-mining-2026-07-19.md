## Headline

Two-part class. First half (~00:00-21:29) is a three-stage striking-defense progression that reads as a leaner, live-taught cousin of the existing `the-square.md` lineage: same core shape (block-only → block+counter → kicks added), but run without a marked perimeter and with a **"4 in the chamber" combo cap** instead of Square's significant-shot counts, and, notably, the **shoot-to-connect-on-the-legs win path is live from Game 1**, not gated behind Square's later clinch/takedown phases. Game 3 also adds a win condition not currently documented anywhere: **catching a kick and off-balancing the kicker's hips or hands to the ground is a full defensive win**, not just the "gold star" bonus that `kick-defense.md` currently gives a caught-and-held leg.

Second half (~31:29-68:45) is the clearest single-class data point yet on how the back-control family chains together: the *same* submit-or-get-on-top / turn-and-face-to-escape ruleset gets run from three different start positions in sequence (seated seatbelt no hooks → turtle with seatbelt-behind-arms → standing Greco with **no grip established at all**), which none of the individual existing docs (`back-maintenance.md`, `back-escape.md`, `turtle-pin.md`, `turtle-breakout.md`) currently frame as one throughline. A student question mid-class also forced an explicit coach ruling on a genuinely ambiguous case: escaping back control into your own guard counts as a win for the escapee even though guard is nominally a worse position in absolute terms, "it's a demotion" for the back-control player specifically, not a neutral outcome for either side.

## Striking / shoot progression (00:00-21:29)

### Game 1 (00:14-11:12): parry-or-block defense vs. combo-or-shoot offense
Offense: up to 3-punch combination (head or body) OR a wrestling shot that connects hands around a leg (above the knee) or the waist under both arms; landing either wins and offense continues. Defense: parry or tight-block only (no slipping, no head movement, limited footwork, "the point is to stay there and defend"); stopping the strike *or* stuffing the shot completely wins and roles switch. This is `tight-block.md` + `parry-the-straight.md` combined with a takedown-stuff option folded in from the very first rep, closer in spirit to `close-range-defense.md`/`the-square.md` but without their marked-perimeter constraint.

**Coaching cue (00:03:36-00:05:30, paraphrased across several exchanges):** offense's real skill is the feint, since a feint sells either the strike or the shot and forces defense to commit; "the more reactions I can get, the more likelihood I can land a shot that's going to land."

### Game 2 (11:12-21:29a, kicks fold in partway through): defense earns a counter
Same combo-or-shoot rules, now raised to a 4-strike max ("you get four in the chamber, that's it"). Defense adds a win condition: successfully block/parry *and* land a counter mid-combination (cannot counter preemptively, that's just striking) = win. A clean full block with zero counter still wins on its own too.

**Coaching cue, "calibration" (00:03:24-00:03:33 relative to this game's debrief):** "There are skills that we're trying to develop in the games... if you find yourself stuck trying the same thing and getting popped four times in a row, adjust the game plan to win. That will help us cycle through, and now you've won once, now let's work your other skill." Explicit statement of the eco-psych principle that constraints exist to develop skills, but *winning* inside a rep still requires reading what's actually working, not forcing a preferred solution. Good concept-page backport candidate if not already captured somewhere.

### Game 3 (kicks added, running into ~21:29): catch-and-dump becomes a new win path
Offense opens up to hands and feet: roundhouse, front/teep kick, leg kicks; explicitly **no kicks to the head** (unless 100% controlled — coach opts out of it entirely today), **no straight kicks to the knee**, **no spinning kicks**. Defense keeps block+counter (now with hands or feet). New addition: **if defense catches a kick and off-balances the kicker so their hips or hands touch the ground, that's a full win** ("hips or hands... hands will also be a loss, if he touches at all"). This is a materially bigger win condition than what's in `kick-defense.md` today, which only credits a caught-and-held leg as a bonus "gold star," not a finishing condition.

**Safety/rules clarification, unprompted (00:04:31-00:04:54 relative):** small-joint manipulation (individual fingers) is illegal; controlling the whole hand is legal. Worth checking this is already stated somewhere in the rules glossary; it came up live because gloves make the distinction ambiguous.

## Back-control progression (31:29-68:45)

All three games below share one ruleset: **offense (the back-control player) wins by finishing a submission (rear-naked choke, armbar, or triangle *only* — leg locks explicitly excluded, "it's either a rear naked choke or an arm bar... or if he falls into some triangle") or by getting to a top-dominant position past the legs (side mount, mount, or belly-down grapevine control). Defense wins simply by turning to face and getting outside back control**, no submission or reversal required, just exit the back-control zone.

### Ground Game A (31:29-42:52): seated back control, no hooks
Start: seated, legs not hooked in, offense has a seatbelt and picks a side. "You don't have to get hooks in, you just need to submit or be on top. You can put hooks in after." Standard back-maintenance/back-escape shape, but the escape criterion and submission menu aren't quite what's on the current pages (see Open Questions #3, #4).

**Explicit coach ruling on an ambiguous case (~48:36, mid-Game-B but answering a question about this rule generally):** *"Coach, if I'm rolling to a guard, how long does he have to be in touch with him?"* → *"You were on offense or defense? If you rolled into guard, you won. You went worse in position. You had his back and now you're in his guard. That's a demotion."* Read both ways: for the **escaping/defending** player, ending up in your own guard counts as a win, position quality doesn't matter, only exiting back control does. For the **back-control/offense** player, losing the back and ending up in the opponent's guard is a loss for *them* specifically, a demotion relative to what they had, even without a full reversal against them.

### Ground Game B (42:52-54:11): same ruleset, from turtle
Start: defender in turtle, top player has a seatbelt behind the arms (any grip they like). Same win conditions as Game A. This is the first time in the transcript history that Back Maintenance/Back Escape's finish logic (submit-or-dominant-position wins, turn-and-face escapes) gets run from a turtle start, which is currently `turtle-pin.md`'s territory, but Turtle Pin's actual finish condition (hips pinned flat ~2-3s) is narrower than what got played live today.

### Ground Game C (54:11-68:45): standing Greco, zero grip established
Start: both players standing, offense's arms fully extended with **no contact yet** ("I'm not cheating by doing this"). Offense must collect a grip fast, off-balance, and either finish or get on top; reaching side mount alone is a win even without a submission. Defense wins by standing and getting away, or, if offense's chase turns into defense getting into a guard, that also wins **unless** offense can pass through it quickly to side mount ("if I'm able to get past him real quick and get into side mount, then I still win, but if he gets a good closed loop around me, then I've lost"). Strikes live throughout; **the wall is explicitly usable** ("you can use the wall to your advantage"). This is functionally a zero-contact precursor to `turtle-breakout.md`, which already starts with the attacker's palms established on the defender's back; nothing in the current library documents the standing, no-grip-yet entry into that same race.

### Closing concepts (63:42-68:45, lecture only, no new game)
- **Weight-reading cue:** "their weight is over the top" is a compromised state defense (bottom) can read and exploit by elevating hips to off-balance the top player; offense's counter is to sit back a bit.
- **High-hip battle:** in scrambles where both players are turning to face each other, whoever gets their hips higher wins the exchange.
- **"Spidey sense" bail decision:** the back-control player needs to recognize in real time when the position is slipping and switch goals from "stay on the back" to "just don't lose the top" ("I gotta switch... know when to make the executive decision to not only bail but ensure that you maintain the top position regardless"). Good concept-page candidate, same shape as the shot-entry cue flagged in the 2026-07-10 report.
- **Escape-route framing:** for chest-to-back defense generally (not just this game), "the escape route is the back door," turn away from where the choking arm is, not into it.
