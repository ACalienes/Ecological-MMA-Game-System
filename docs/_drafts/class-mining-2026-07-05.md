2. **Knee-on-belly / north-south escapes: knee-on-belly, yes; north-south, not yet.** He likes a knee-on-belly game built around the top player floating/maintaining the position while the bottom player off-balances and tries to recover guard or stand, close to my sketch. Open question, not a build-it-today answer.
3. **Transition Zone is 100% defensive: agrees, and says the fix is really the same game from the other side.** His framing: "the defense for something is the offense for something else," so the fix isn't a brand-new game, it's re-activating an offense-side framing of a connection-forcing game that already exists in spirit. He described almost exactly what got played as Game 1 today (see below): offense voluntarily forces a real clinch/tie connection (any type, even a leg), defense uses footwork and jab/teep to deny it, either in open space or at the wall.
4. **Skill-isolation is 100% striking: intentional, not an oversight, given the gym.** Same jiu-jitsu-dominant-gym reasoning as #1, the JJ classes already own wrestling/grappling skill-isolation. He raised a sharper definitional point worth keeping: a true "skill-isolation wrestling" game would need to be wrestling-only, not MMA-flavored, the same way his striking-isolation games are boxing-only, not MMA-flavored. He wants to think this through theoretically rather than test it live, since it doesn't serve this specific gym's students right now.
5. **Wall has no submission-offense page: reframed as correct as-is, not a gap.** His read: the wall is genuinely a rare place for offense to *finish* a submission in a real fight, the offense's job at the wall is almost always to pin or ground someone, not choke them there. The one real exception is a standing guillotine/neck-choke where the *defender* traps the offense's neck against the cage, which is already the defensive side, already covered by `wall-defensive-submission.md`. ## Class content mined (4 blocks)

### Block 1 (16:43-36:26): Force-the-connection game, offense=lead jab only, defense=lead hand + teeps → then defense=rear hand only + teeps
Open space. Offense wins by establishing *any* meaningful clinch connection (50/50, plum, body lock, even a leg grab counts) such that the defender can't pull away, offense is restricted to the lead-hand jab only (can switch stance, straights only). Defense's job is to deny the connection using footwork and hand control; first run defense has the lead hand "however they want" plus teeps (any front kick) off either leg; the escalation restricts defense to the *rear* hand only plus teeps. Coaching notes: threaten a level change even when the win doesn't require a takedown, to make the defender's job harder; "the environment is always a factor," the wall cuts both ways; win-the-circle / cut-the-cage cueing recurs throughout.

**Maps to `pressure-to-clinch.md`'s existing "Lead-Leg Barrier" variant** (built 2026-06-15), and matches its documented win condition almost exactly (get past the elbows into a clinch, any 50/50, both hands under, or a leg connection). **What's new:** the existing ladder only ever gives the *attacker* progressively more weapons (no weapons → may reach → gains the rear cross) while the defender gains tools going the other way (teeps → +lead jab). Today's session ran a rung that isn't in the documented ladder: attacker fixed at lead-jab-only (a real weapon, short of the rear cross) while the *defender's* tool got restricted to one specific hand (rear only) rather than expanded. That's a genuinely new axis, restricting the defender's tool instead of only expanding the attacker's, worth adding to the existing variant rather than a new page.

### Block 2 (36:26-58:43): Leg entanglement / MMA leg-lock game
Start in a standing leg entanglement (any control on the bottom leg/hip, over-under or scooped, whatever's comfortable). **Bottom player's win:** get the top player's hips to the ground and come up on top (a full reversal), or create enough separation to just stand and disengage, or (advanced players only) finish a leg submission. **Top player has no win condition by design**, their job is to strip the entanglement and land realistic ground-and-pound while staying on their feet (can't pass guard, can't sit down into it, must keep feet planted, can use hands to post/off-balance but not to bear weight). If the entanglement breaks, they re-entangle and restart. Explicit rule: bottom getting top's hands down onto the mat isn't itself a win, but it matters because a top player with weight-loaded hands physically can't strike, so it's a real damage-mitigation tool for bottom. Second run added: strikes explicitly live for both, and the "if top gives you too much space, you can just stand up and win" rule restated more plainly.

Coaching cues: the standing leg-entanglement mechanic itself (one foot to the opponent's hip, the other controlling their ankle, "lever from the top to the bottom of the leg"), the pendulum off-balancing technique (push their weight one way, they compensate, drag it back the other way, repeat until they can't recover), collecting the bottom's *other* leg (the one they're posting on to stay upright) as a second target alongside hand-to-mat. Design rationale in his own words: jiu-jitsu players coming into MMA often think they'll leg-lock people and don't realize they'll get smashed while trying, this game is built specifically to teach both roles what that trade actually feels like.

**No existing page covers this.** This is a real new game, and a richer, more specific version than my Friday draft sketch ("Leg Entanglement, Position Only," symmetric roles, no submissions at all). Alex's live version is asymmetric (bottom = escape/reversal/advanced-submission, top = GnP-and-strip with deliberately no win condition) and does allow submissions for advanced players. Recommend building from *this* version, not my draft.

### Block 3 (58:43-1:07:04): 50/50 escape variant
Starts in the neutral 50/50 tie. One player (offense/"clincher") is trying to progress: hips-down, past 50/50 into a double-under, or connect on the leg. The other (defense/"non-clincher") is trying to separate completely, win = clean separation. Sweeps are live for both, offense sweeps to win, defense sweeps to escape. Wall is usable. Strikes explicitly allowed from the tie.

**Maps to `fifty-fifty-clinch.md`**, which already documents the same win paths (double-unders locked, leg above the knee, the back, off-balance-to-ground-while-standing) and the same wall/strikes levels. **What's different:** the existing page is a symmetric race, both fighters have the identical goal, first to a dominant connection. Today's version is asymmetric, one player's whole job is to separate and escape rather than out-connect the other. That's a distinct framing (attacker-vs-escaper, not a mutual race) worth adding as a named variant, not folded silently into the existing levels.

### Block 4 (1:07:46-1:15:24): Legs-only live sparring
Freestyle situational sparring, kicks above the waist only count as strikes (teeps, roundhouses, side kicks), hands stay fully live but only for catching kicks, clinching, and distance management, not as striking weapons. Takedowns reset to standing. Purpose: get live kicking reps inside real freestyle sparring rather than isolated kick drilling. Correction mid-round: some players weren't using the hands they were explicitly allowed (catching, clinching) and got overwhelmed as a result, a reminder that "you have more tools live than you're using" is itself a coaching point worth keeping.

**No existing page or format matches this exactly.** It's close in spirit to the "Full MMA" capstone levels already documented on `lead-leg-offense.md` / `rear-leg-offense.md`, but as a standalone live-sparring format (one weapon class live, everything else present but non-lethal) rather than a level on an isolation page. Worth a naming decision, its own small page vs. a shared "weapon-restricted live sparring" format the way Win Ladder is a shared format.

## Reusable coaching gold

1. **"Stop the bleeding" / damage control** (1:15:32-1:18:11): if you genuinely can't win a game by winning on its actual terms, the mindset shifts to losing by as little as possible, e.g. eating a kick and holding the clinch to stall a kick-heavy opponent's rhythm, then moving again the moment they disengage. Explicitly generalized: applies across many games, not just the one it came up in. Strong standalone concept-page candidate (same shape as "Winning the Circle").
2. **Pendulum off-balancing** (56:02-57:25): get their weight moving one way, they compensate, then take it back the other way, repeat until recovery fails. Demonstrated live on the leg-entanglement game but generic to any off-balancing problem.
3. **Foot positioning / centerline access** (1:23:45-1:26:01): a genuine Q&A explainer on inside vs. outside foot position in a striking stance, outside foot gives you punch access to their centerline they don't have to yours; inside foot trades that for other options (different kick lines, closer shot access). Alex's take: roughly a 70/30 default toward outside position, not an absolute rule, framed explicitly as "not a rule, a tendency." Good concept-page or glossary material.
4. **Read-and-adjust in sparring** (1:28:05-1:29:55): a student defaulting to "throw the cross, and stop thinking" got coached to notice the opponent's habitual counter (a check hook to the body every time) and adjust the next rep, generalized as picking up patterns rather than only executing a plan A with no plan B/C/D.
