## Progression mined (5 stages, ~00:00-00:54)

### Stage 1 (00:00-00:09:44): Lead-hand-only slip, open space — matches Slip the Straight closely
Offense: lead-hand jab only, head only, no body shots, open space (not boxed), continuous "winner stays in." Offense's stated goal is volume ("land as many times as you can"), which Alex frames as intentionally too easy on its own, existing to give defense a live launching pad. Defense: parry/block until comfortable, then look for one clean slip to win and switch roles. Explicit rule (00:03:49-00:04:08): leaning back or retreating while slipping does **not** count as a win, must slip forward/offline while staying in stance. This is the exact mechanic already documented on `slip-the-straight.md` ("no block, no duck, no backing up"). **Good confirmation data, no gap.**

A side tangent (00:06:35-00:09:00, not game content): Alex flags that pad-holders among the students aren't holding pads with the eco-principles in mind ("in order to do pad holding the right way... I need to ensure anybody who holds pads understands the concepts, or else we're just doing a cardio workout"). Not library material, but worth surfacing as a coaching-education gap he raised himself.

### Stage 2 (00:09:53-00:19:48): Boxed to one square + legs go live for defense
Same slip-to-win rule, now confined to a single marked square (removes the open-space escape route). New rule added at 00:11:22-00:11:34: **"At any point on defense, I can also shoot to connect onto the leg."** This persists as a standing option through the rest of the striking progression (restated in Stages 3 and 4). **This is the standout new mechanic** — see Headline. Immediately after (00:11:34-00:12:00), Alex refines the anti-retreat rule rather than contradicting it: movement is fine, "it needs to be strategic movement, not evasive movement... not evading, retreating movement," i.e., repositioning is allowed, running away is not. Anti-retreat rule reinforced a second time at 00:13:49-00:13:55. Mid-stage coaching correction on student caution (00:17:33-00:17:57): "you're not risking the chance to slip, you're just pairing everything... try more."

### Stage 3 (00:19:48-00:35:11): Lead + rear mixed, single shots only, counter-off-slip becomes a bonus
Both hands live but single strikes only, no combinations. Explicit anti-hyper-feint coaching (00:20:11-00:20:23): "I don't want us feinting heavy... I want solid biophysical markers that show jab is coming," i.e., a feint should read as a committed strike, not a twitch. Countering after a successful slip is now a bonus ("gold star") but not required to win. Legs-live option restated (00:20:55-00:21:04).

**Coaching aside inside this stage (00:26:29-00:29:01), wrestling shot mechanics:** don't reach for a leg with an extended arm from range (over-committing, telegraphed); instead collapse full body level while staying "loaded" (Ilya Topuria cited as the model), which is just as threatening as a real shot without committing, and use the opponent's flinch/reaction to that fake level-change as the green light to actually go ("if I did that once, then that should be my green light that... I need to go, the door was open"). Standalone technical cue, not a game constraint, good backport candidate.

**Divergence at 00:35:11-00:35:28:** Alex offers to switch the back half of class to a wrestling concept ("I kind of had like a wrestling concept to round the games out"), students explicitly vote to keep going with the striking progression instead ("let's keep doing this because I like, I see progress"), and Alex follows that read rather than his own plan.

### Stage 4 (00:35:30-00:53:16): One-hand offense, counter now mandatory; cross-side variant layered in near the end
Offense drops to a single hand (lead or rear, picked freely). Defense's win condition upgrades from "slip" to **"evade and counter"**, pure evasion no longer wins alone (00:35:53-00:36:07). The anti-retreat rule is explicitly loosened here: "I don't care if you lean back and counter, I don't care if you do a 360," any evasion method is fine as long as a counter follows. Feinting becomes more central since offense only has one tool (00:36:38-00:36:46). Legs-live option persists throughout (00:36:27-00:36:35, 00:49:02-00:49:25). At 00:48:52-00:49:25 a cross-side variant layers onto this same mandatory-counter rule: offense restricted to the cross only, defense keeps the jab, same evade-and-counter win condition, same legs-live option.

**Wall/perimeter-awareness teaching moment (00:50:58-00:52:05):** offense's job in this stage becomes pushing defense out of the square; defense near the edge has to choose jab or level-change. Alex generalizes past the stated rule, observing a student instinctively using the wall to trap an opponent even in a stage without a stated wall mechanic, into an explicit standing principle: "the second that we start playing, it's like, I don't care what the rules are... you should always be playing the environment to your advantage." Coach-intent catching up to student behavior, a genuine divergence-is-the-signal moment.
